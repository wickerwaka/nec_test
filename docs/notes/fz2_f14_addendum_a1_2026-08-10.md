## 1. WHAT WAS MISSING

The brief's step 5 says *"the corpus's socket goldens are the chip and reproduce
per the C-9 precedent; the fabric leg is what moves"*, and step 6 asks for *"the
golden set on the new capture"*.  The pre-registration recorded the intent and
**never turned it into a bar**.  That is a hole, because it is the assumption
every seat prediction rests on:

> An fz2 capture has two legs from the same physical part in the same socket —
> `real`, the socketed chip, and `sim`, the fabric core.  **Every number this
> sitting reports is a difference between them.**  If the chip leg moved across
> the re-flash, then nothing that moved may be charged to the five landings, and
> the whole seat-by-seat comparison is uninterpretable.

C-9 measured stability **on one flash** (192 seeds × 3 repeats, rows AND arch,
`stable: 192`).  It says nothing about a re-flash.  The mechanism says the chip
should not move — the harness RTL around the socket is unchanged between two
ucore bitstreams and the images are regenerated from the same frozen seeds — but
**that is an argument, and this sitting is supposed to measure it.**

**Acceptance: 3,840 / 3,840 on every field of (a) and 725 / 725 on (b).**

**IF `C-CHIP` FAILS, IT IS A HARD STOP FOR ATTRIBUTION, NOT FOR THE SITTING.**
The capture is still valid data and the headline is still a true silicon-match
figure.  What is void is the *seat-by-seat attribution to the five landings*,
because a moved chip leg means the two corpora were scored against different
references.  In that case: report the headline, report the C-CHIP failure with
its seed list, and **make no per-landing closure claim** — do not "correct" for
it, and do not swap to the archived chip rows, which would be choosing a
reference after seeing a result.

**A PARTIAL FAILURE IS ALSO A RESULT.**  If (a) passes and (b) fails on a
handful of seeds, that is the interesting case — the arch dump and the
terminator agreed while the row stream did not — and it must be itemized seed by
seed rather than summarised, because it would be a rig-timing finding of the
same class as C-6's row-sha caveat.

**IT IS NOT SCORED THE WAY C-6 IS.**  f13 §10.4 established that the C-6 control
probe's raw row sha256 is *not* a reproducible quantity, because that probe
captures a free-running spin program.  An fz2 seed is not that: it is a
deterministic program with a rig-scheduled event and a terminating NMI, its rows
are already compared row for row by the corpus's own comparator, and C-9 found
them stable across three repeats.  **The two situations are different and the
C-6 caveat does not extend to C-CHIP.**
